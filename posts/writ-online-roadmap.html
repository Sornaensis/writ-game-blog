The Writ Online roadmap

Writ Online is a persistent, simulated world. Important persons and player characters are simulated continuously. They have daily needs, schedules, goals of their own, and can form parties, trade, work, and much more. The game also simulates large populations in an efficient way, to keep the world consistent to its scale goal, which is on the order of thousands of square kilometers.

This roadmap is a high-level outline of the development process' progress.

1. The simulation engine

The deterministic core everything else runs on.

  • Second-by-second simulated time, with exact catch-up when nobody is watching
  • Real physical units for mass, volume, energy and time throughout
  • Deterministic randomness: the same world replays the same way, even across save and restore
  • Clear separation between "you can't do that" and genuine faults
  • Everything in the world defined as readable content
    • Races, items, recipes, buildings, occupations and places
    • Layered scenarios on top of a shared base
    • Selectable rule profiles for different worlds and servers
  • Performance work so larger populations run at full speed (in progress)

2. World, geography and places

The land itself, and the places people live and travel.

  • Hand-authored persistent worlds with terrain, biomes and settlements
  • Hex-based geography with local daylight and seasons
  • Detailed local places that persist and change between visits
    • Homes, workshops, farms and wells
    • Settlement-owned farm plots and production sites
  • Cooperative playtest scenarios and a six-settlement demo region
  • Ecology
    • Water that flows and runs out
    • Fishing waters
    • Herds and wildlife
    • Finite natural resources
  • Movement and travel
    • Walking between places for shelter, warmth, refuge and exploration
    • Roads and hex improvements
    • Carrying loads, and camps
    • Travelling with dependents
  • Underground places: caves, wells, ruins and artifacts

3. People and bodies

Every person is a body with needs, not a bag of hit points.

  • Physiology
    • Hunger, thirst, fatigue and rest
    • Exertion from work, charged the same way for one person or a whole crew
    • Temperature and shelter
  • Deprivation and death by cause
  • First aid, treatment and recovery
  • Character creation rooted in where and to whom you're born
    • Life histories and natural lifespans for each ancestry
  • Skills that grow with practice
  • Injuries from the environment and from dangerous work
  • Illness, poison and self-diagnosis
  • Deeper medicine and long-term care

4. Families and populations

The whole population is simulated, not just the people a player can see.

  • Partnership, marriage, pregnancy and birth
  • Children who grow up within families, and adoption
  • Group simulation of large populations
    • Groups defined by ancestry, culture, faith and station, with age bands and a sex ratio
    • Shared group bodies that eat, drink, lodge and suffer from climate and shortage
    • Deaths by real cause at group scale
    • People drawn out as individuals when it matters: meeting a player, marrying, holding office or property
    • People returned to their group, still identified, when they're no longer needed in detail
    • Group members who stay named and keep their relationships while being simulated cheaply
    • Group occupational work
      • Job holders assigned to settlement workshops and fields automatically
      • Crews sized by who holds the trade and limited by what each place can hold
      • Crew work and tiredness charged exactly as for an individual
      • Farming takes priority when crops need tending
    • Group births, aging and households
    • Migration between settlements
    • Learning a trade by working in a group

5. Livelihoods and daily life

What people actually do with their days.

  • Daily routines: sleep, work, meals, shopping, socialising and travel
  • Personal goals pursued by the people themselves
    • Earn a living, and find food and water
    • Study, teach, research and map the land
    • Barter, make friends and seek revenge
    • Marry, and have or adopt children
    • Decipher texts and keep archives
    • Flee danger
    • Pilgrimages, keepsakes, grief and gatherings
  • Occupations that do real work: smiths, woodcutters, farmers, charcoal burners and more
  • More trades
    • Fishing and herding
    • Masonry and pottery
    • Standing watch and patrol
    • Leading worship
  • Commuting to work outside town, and personal time
  • Investing in someone else's business
  • Settlement leaders who decide what gets built and produced

6. Crafting, buildings and property

  • Multi-step production chains: ore and charcoal into metal into tools
  • Crafting quality that depends on skill, materials and tools
  • Repair, alchemy and preparation
  • Homes, hearths and household fuel
  • Settlement production
    • Settlement-owned workshops run batches from the settlement's own stock
    • Settlement farm plots planted, tended and harvested by local crews
    • Furnaces and other processes that need tending over time
  • Building, maintaining and upgrading structures and infrastructure
  • Stoves, cooking and fuel management at home

7. Trade and economy

  • Barter between residents, driven by real need
  • Local markets and prices
  • Settlement stockpiles that feed production and the population
  • Inventories, containers, spoilage and storage
  • Cargo, caravans and long-distance logistics
  • Contracts, debt and shared ventures
  • Regional economies that rise and fall with supply

8. Companions and parties

  • Travelling in parties
  • Companions who craft and carry for the group
  • Missions and party duties
  • Companions with their own goals and loyalties

9. Knowledge, culture and society

People know only what they have seen, heard or been told.

  • Perception-based knowledge: nobody is omniscient
  • Letters and maps that carry real information
  • Relationships and opinions formed through real interaction
  • Living languages that drift and evolve over time (in progress)
  • Reputation built from what people actually witness (in progress)
  • Authored cultures with their own customs and languages
  • Dialogue, fame and quests that emerge from the world
  • Religion: practices, holy sites, clergy and schisms

10. Law, politics and institutions

  • Law, procedure and enforcement: watchmen, courts and punishment
  • Membership, offices and titles
  • Land grants, delegation and taxation
  • Estates, governing procedures and succession
  • Diplomacy, intrigue and political crises
  • National control over territory

11. Conflict and war

  • Contact-based combat with protection, equipment and handling (in progress)
  • Moving unseen, and fleeing (in progress)
  • Tactical actions, formations and combat decisions
  • Mobilisation, supply lines and campaigns
  • Captivity, ransom and sieges

12. Death, inheritance and continuation

Death is permanent, but your story doesn't have to end.

  • Death by real causes, recorded in the world's history
  • Inheritance of property and titles
  • Continuing play through heirs and descendants
  • Legacies that shape the world after a character is gone

13. Playing online

Writ is built to run as a durable multiplayer world.

  • A server that runs the world continuously, with a player API
    • Each player sees only what their character can perceive
    • Contextual actions that explain exactly why something can't be done
    • Full end-to-end play and recovery through the player API (in progress)
  • Invite-only registration with revocable credentials
  • A local, read-only observer view for watching a world run
  • The Writ Online game client (design in progress)
  • Fallback routines that keep your character fed and rested while you're away
  • A regional demo world, and long-running world observation spanning months of game time

14. Deployment and operations

  • A self-contained server container image
  • Health and readiness reporting, with a clean shutdown that never loses state
  • Saves with full history, backup and restore
  • Bounded load handling that refuses work explicitly rather than stalling
  • Saving every mechanism's in-flight state, so any world resumes exactly where it stopped
  • An automated playthrough and disaster-recovery harness
  • Performance and scaling targets for a fully living world

This post is updated continuously as the Roadmap Expands and items are completed.
